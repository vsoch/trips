Each route has its own map, an ordered list of stops, and a few things worth learning along the way.

Before you head out

  • Fog & layers. The marine layer can turn a warm valley into a cold ridge in minutes. Bring a wind layer even on clear mornings, especially for Mt Tam and the coast.
  • Muir Woods needs a reservation. Parking and shuttle must be booked in advance at gomuirwoods.com — check current rules before the Dipsea hike.
  • Tides & time. Tennessee Cove, Pirates Cove and the tide pools are best (and safest) at low tide. Never turn your back on the surf here — sneaker waves are real.
  • Waterfalls are seasonal. Cataract Falls is spectacular after winter/spring rain and can be a trickle by late summer.
  • Ride mornings. Bay wind builds in the afternoon; the Paradise and Sausalito loops are calmest before noon. Watch for tourist foot traffic in Sausalito and on the bridge path.

About the maps

Pins mark the key stops in order; the dashed line shows the sequence, not a turn-by-turn GPS track. Use the “Open turn-by-turn directions” button on each route for live navigation (walking for hikes, cycling for rides). A few backcountry waypoints are approximate — trust trail signs and a dedicated app (AllTrails, Gaia) over these markers once you're out there. Base map: OpenTopoMap (contours + trails); switch layers with the control in each map's corner.
